# Feedback Synthesizer

Turn scattered feedback into bounded patterns while preserving minority findings, contradictions and sampling limits.

## Resolve the dataset

Ask where authorised interviews, tickets, reviews, surveys, NPS responses, sales or onboarding notes, churn records, test feedback and community comments are stored. If access is unavailable, request the material.

Define the analysis question, dataset, date range, customer or participant segments and known collection limitations before generalising.

## Apply enhanced Aletheia controls

Give every source item and material theme a stable ID. Type inputs and conclusions as `Evidence`, `Observation`, `Measurement`, `Estimate`, `Claim`, `Hypothesis`, `Conflict` or `EvidenceGap`.

Record channel, creator or speaker role, knowledge basis, source-created or event date, retrieval date, locator, claim scope, limitations, quotation or consent restrictions and privacy class.

Score each theme `relevance: 0–10` against the stated question and explain scores of 7 or above. Keep frequency, severity, strategic relevance and truth confidence separate.

Detect contradictions across segments, periods, product versions, locations and channels. Do not average them away. Create a conflict entry showing each material side and possible contextual cause without declaring that cause established.

Report `integrity`, `source_identity`, `epistemic_support`, `applicability` and `completeness` separately, with reasons. State sampling and coverage gaps and leave unknown values unknown.

Date every theme and test whether older feedback has been superseded by a product, policy or service change. Archive superseded findings rather than presenting them as current.

Create an interpretation receipt for every consequential theme or recommendation: supporting items, inclusion and exclusion criteria, alternatives, applicability, conflicts and evidence that would change the conclusion.

## Synthesise

Identify repeated pain points, feature or service requests, objections, adoption barriers, positive drivers and customer language. Weight independent repetition more than intensity from one source. Use quotations only when authorised and anonymise personal or sensitive details.

## Output

1. Executive summary.
2. Dataset and limitations.
3. Top pain points and friction.
4. Top requests.
5. Positive sentiment drivers.
6. Customer or participant language.
7. Conflicts and segment differences.
8. Recommended actions tied to evidence.
9. Provenance trace, evidence gaps and human-review points.
